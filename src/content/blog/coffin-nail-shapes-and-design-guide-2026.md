Bold, dramatic, and unmistakably architectural, **Coffin (also known as Ballerina) nails** remain the reigning silhouette for high-impact glamour and social-first nail art in 2026.

Mimicking the elegant taper of a ballerina's pointe shoe or the sharp edges of a classic coffin, this shape features straight sidewalls tapering inward toward a crisp, flat horizontal tip. In this definitive shape masterclass, our master sculptors explore the precision filing angles, structural C-curve reinforcement, and design ideas that make coffin sets durable and stunning.

> **TL;DR / Quick Answer:** To shape coffin nails: file straight sidewalls at a 30-degree inward taper, hold your file perpendicular at 90 degrees to square off the flat tip, and reinforce the stress apex with builder gel or acrylic to prevent sidewall cracking. For a detailed comparison between coffin and almond silhouettes, read our [Almond vs. Coffin Shape Comparison Guide](/blog/aesthetic-almond-and-coffin-nail-designs-guide-2026/).

## The Precision Coffin Filing Protocol

```text
Step 1: File Straight Sidewall Taper ➔ Step 2: 90° Square Free Edge ➔ Step 3: Check C-Curve Pinch ➔ Step 4: Refine Sharp Corners
```

1. **The 30-Degree Sidewall Angle:** Place a 180-grit file along the lateral edge. File straight inward at approximately 30 degrees toward the center axis. Ensure the file remains completely flat against the edge—do not rock the file or round the corners.
2. **The 90-Degree Square Tip:** Hold your file completely vertical, perpendicular (90 degrees) to the finger. Make firm, horizontal passes across the tip to create a crisp, flat edge.
3. **Pinch the C-Curve (For Acrylic/Hard Gel):** During the gel/acrylic curing phase, use pinching tongs to compress the sidewalls by 30–40%. This creates an arch that gives coffin nails their structural resilience.
4. **De-burr Without Rounding:** Run a soft buffer across the sharp bottom edge to eliminate rough burrs while preserving the crisp geometry.

## Preventing Coffin Sidewall Fractures

Because coffin nails have sharp 90-degree corners, they experience concentrated mechanical leverage when typing or opening doors:

1. **Never File into the Natural Sidewall Groove:** Keep filing strictly on the free edge extension to avoid thinning the natural nail plate's lateral growth points.
2. **Daily Cuticle Oiling:** Hydrated extensions flex slightly upon impact instead of snapping. Follow our [Cuticle Care Routine](/blog/cuticle-care-and-nail-hydration-routine/).
3. **If Lifting Occurs:** Follow our [DIY Nail Lifting Troubleshooting Guide](/blog/diy-nail-lifting-troubleshooting-and-prevention/) immediately to prevent trapped moisture pockets.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you do coffin nails on natural nails without extensions?

Yes, but only if your natural nails have at least 5–7mm of free edge length and are reinforced with a structured [Builder Gel (BIAB) Overlay](/blog/how-to-do-builder-gel-overlay-at-home/) to prevent corner chipping.

### What is the difference between Ballerina and Coffin shapes?

They are essentially identical in modern salon terminology. Historically, "Ballerina" referred to tips with slightly softer, curved sidewalls before squaring off, while "Coffin" denoted completely straight, sharp tapered lines.

### Are coffin nails difficult to type with?

Long coffin nails require typing with the flat pads of your fingers rather than fingertips. For heavy typing jobs, choose a "Short Coffin" length or switch to [Almond Shapes](/blog/almond-shape-nail-designs-and-styling-guide/).
